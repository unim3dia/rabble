A living pile of little clay people, awake together.

Drop into the pile, look around, zoom right in, tap someone and they bop. The pile talks about real things happening today, and when you say something, the people next to you answer.

Made with Meta AI

Three Meta models do the work, each where it's best.

Muse Image

Every person

Drew all 47 characters, ages 10 to 90, in up to seven poses each: awake, reaching, dozing. It also turns your own photo into a little clay version of you.

SAM 3.1

Every cutout

Cuts every figure out of its background, and cuts you out of your photo, then out of your new clay self.

Muse Spark

All the talk

Searches the web for today's real news and writes tiny conversations: one smart line, one funny, one a little perturbed. It rewords what you say, kindly, and answers you.

How it plays

On a phone, in about a minute.

  1. The pile. A leaning tower of people with a wide, steady base. Drag to turn it, pinch to zoom anywhere. Newcomers fall from the sky and land on top.
  2. Become one of them. Use your photo and you get your own clay person. The photo is never saved.
  3. Drop in. You fall into the pile and it catches you. A marker shows where you are; press and drag yourself to a new spot.
  4. Widen the ring. Neighborhood, city, state, world: more people pile on and the light of the room changes.
  5. Say something. Your words heap up as you type; the people beside you answer.
  6. One shared pile. Everyone who opens the link is in the same pile and sees what everyone says.

What's next

AI conversation for the sake of connection and play: the pile introducing strangers to each other, talking about real places and real news near them, and asking playful questions that get people talking to one another, not just to the AI.

This page runs the pile without a server: its talk is a night of conversations Muse Spark wrote ahead of time, and making your own clay person is turned off. The full version (your photo, live AI replies, everyone in one shared pile) runs on the Rabble server in the repository.
